# TOFTOptionsReceiverModule miss cross-chain

## Description

Cross-chain token decimals transformation is applied partially in TOFTOptionsReceiverModule's lockAndParticipateReceiver() and mintLendXChainSGLXChainLockAndParticipateReceiver(). Currently only first level amounts are being transformed in cross-chain TOFTOptionsReceiverModule, while the nested deposit and lock amounts involved aren't. Whenever the decimals are different for underlying tokens across chains the absence of transformation will lead to magnitudes sized misrepresentation of user operations, which can result in core functionality unavailability (operations can constantly revert or become a noops due to running them with outsized or dust sized parameters) and loss of user funds (when an operation was successfully run, but with severely misrepresented parameters). Probability can be estimated as medium due to prerequisite of having asset decimals difference between transacting chains, while the operation misrepresentation and possible fund loss impact described itself has high severity. ## Derived Narrative

The following field is derived content and may not be source-grounded:

The vulnerability is a cross‑chain decimal‑scaling mismatch in the TOFTOptionsReceiverModule. When a user initiates a cross‑chain operation that involves minting, depositing collateral, or locking option tokens, the contract converts the primary amount (for example, mintAmount or fraction) from the source chain’s token decimals to the destination chain’s local decimals using the internal _toLD helper. However, the nested amount fields – such as the collateralDepositData.amount inside the mintData structure, and the amount and fraction fields inside the lockData structure – are left unchanged. This partial conversion occurs only at the first level of the calldata, so any underlying token whose decimal representation differs between the two chains is processed with an incorrect magnitude. The root cause is an oversight in the receiver functions lockAndParticipateReceiver() and mintLendXChainSGLXChainLockAndParticipateReceiver(), where the developer applied the decimal transformation to a single field but omitted the same transformation for all other amount fields contained in the same message.

An attacker (or any user) can exploit this by submitting a cross‑chain request involving a token with mismatched decimals. Because the nested amounts are not scaled, the destination contract interprets them as either enormously large values or as dust‑sized values. In the former case, the operation may revert due to overflow checks or because the protocol rejects parameters that exceed allowed limits. In the latter case, the operation may succeed but effectively lock or mint a negligible amount, resulting in a no‑op from the user’s perspective. Both scenarios break core functionality: legitimate operations may constantly revert, and successful calls may lead to a loss of user funds when the protocol records an incorrect amount that cannot be redeemed.

The impact is high. Users see their expected balances disappear or remain unchanged after a transaction that appeared to succeed. From the UI perspective, a user may observe that the mint amount displayed matches their input, yet the collateral deposit or locked amount shows as zero or an unexpectedly tiny value. The protocol’s accounting assumptions – that the sum of minted tokens, deposited collateral, and locked amounts are consistent across chains – are violated, leading to accounting gaps and potential fund loss.

The issue manifests only when the source and destination chains use tokens with different decimal configurations and when the operation includes nested amount fields (collateral deposit or option lock). It was discovered during a manual audit by the researcher Sherlock, who noted that the conversion logic was applied selectively. The bug is subtle because the top‑level amount appears correct, masking the incorrect handling of deeper fields, making it hard to detect through simple testing that only checks the primary amount.

To remediate, the receiver module should apply the decimal conversion to every amount field present in the incoming message: the mintAmount, the collateralDepositData.amount, the lockData.amount, and the lockData.fraction. By ensuring that all numeric values are normalized to the destination chain’s decimals before any state‑changing calls, the protocol will preserve the intended economic values and prevent the described reverts, no‑ops, and fund loss.
